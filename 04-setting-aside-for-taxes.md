# Setting aside for taxes

Move a percentage of every payment you receive into a separate tax holdback account the moment it arrives. The single worst cash surprise for a solo operator is a tax bill on money already spent. A holdback account prevents it.

## Why a holdback account

When you are an employee, taxes are withheld before you see the money. When you work for yourself, the full payment lands in your account and the tax on it is your responsibility to set aside. It is easy to treat the whole amount as available, spend it, and then face a bill you no longer have the cash for. A separate holdback account keeps the tax portion out of your spendable balance from the start.

## How to do it

Open a second account for taxes. Each time income arrives, transfer a set percentage of it into the holdback account and do not touch that money. When tax payments are due, the money is already there. The discipline is to move the percentage immediately and consistently, treating it as money that was never yours to spend.

## What percentage

The right percentage depends on your income, location, entity, and other factors, so the specific number is a question for a tax professional. The principle is to estimate conservatively and set aside enough that the bill is covered, adjusting as you learn your actual rate. Setting aside slightly too much is a minor inconvenience. Setting aside too little is the surprise you are trying to avoid.

## Estimated payments

In many places, self-employed people are expected to pay tax periodically through the year rather than once at the end, and missing those can add penalties. Whether this applies to you and on what schedule is jurisdiction-specific and worth confirming. The holdback account makes whatever schedule applies far easier, because the money is set aside and ready when each due date arrives.

## Treat it as not your money

The mental shift that makes this work is to stop seeing the tax portion as income. From the moment a payment arrives, a slice of it belongs to the tax bill, not to you. Moving it to a separate account immediately turns that idea into a habit, so your spendable balance only ever shows what is truly yours.
