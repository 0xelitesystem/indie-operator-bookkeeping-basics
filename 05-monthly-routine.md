# Monthly routine

Once a month, sit down for about half an hour and bring your books current: match transactions to your records, categorize everything, file receipts, and look at the month's income and expenses. A short, consistent monthly close beats a frantic annual scramble every time.

## The monthly close, step by step

1. Pull the month's business bank and card statements. These are your source of truth.
2. Match every transaction to your records, adding any you missed. Each line should have a date, amount, party, category, and note.
3. Categorize anything uncategorized, using your small set of categories.
4. File the receipts that back up expenses, organized by month.
5. Confirm your tax holdback transfers happened for the month's income.
6. Look at the totals: income, expenses, and what is left. Note anything surprising.

## Why monthly, not yearly

Records are easiest to reconstruct while they are fresh. A charge from last week is obvious; a charge from eleven months ago is a mystery. Doing this monthly keeps each session small and the details recoverable. Saving it all for tax season turns bookkeeping into a multi-day reconstruction of a year you no longer remember clearly.

## Reconcile against the bank

The core of the close is reconciliation: making your records match the bank statement, line for line. When they match, you know your books are complete and accurate for the month. When they do not, the mismatch is a missing or wrong entry to fix while it is still findable. Reconciliation is what turns a list of guesses into trustworthy books.

## Watch the trend, not just the month

Once you have a few months closed, look across them. Is income steady, growing, lumpy? Are expenses creeping up? Is one category bigger than you thought? The monthly numbers are useful on their own, but the trend across months is where you actually see the business, and it costs nothing extra once you are closing each month anyway.

## Keep it boring and repeatable

The value of the routine is that it is the same every month and small enough that you will actually do it. Resist turning it into an elaborate system. A consistent half hour with a statement, your records, and your categories keeps a solo operation's books in good shape with no drama.
