# What to track

Track income, expenses, and the date and purpose of each, plus anything you might need to prove later. You do not need an elaborate system. You need a consistent record of money in, money out, and enough detail that a stranger could understand each line.

## The short list

For every business transaction, capture the date, the amount, who it was with, and what it was for. That is the core. Income: when, how much, from whom, for what. Expenses: when, how much, to whom, for what category. If you have those four facts for every line, you have functional books.

## Keep the receipts that matter

Keep records that back up your expenses, especially larger ones and anything that could be questioned. Digital copies are fine and easier to keep than paper. A folder of receipts organized by month, matched to your bank statement, is enough for most solo operators. The point is to be able to show what a charge was, not to file beautifully.

## Categories, kept simple

Group expenses into a small set of categories that match how your business actually spends: software and subscriptions, contractors, fees, equipment, marketing, and so on. A handful of clear categories beats dozens of precise ones you will not maintain. Categories exist to answer questions like where the money goes and what is deductible, so pick ones that serve those questions.

## What you can usually skip

You do not need to track personal spending in your business books, because it should not be in the business account at all. You do not need minute-by-minute precision on tiny amounts. And you do not need accounting software on day one; a simple spreadsheet with date, amount, party, category, and a note works until volume justifies a tool.

## Track income gross, note the fees

When a platform or processor takes a cut, record the full amount you earned and the fee as an expense, rather than only the net that landed. This keeps your real revenue visible and the fees countable, which matters for understanding margins and for taxes. Netting them silently hides both numbers.
