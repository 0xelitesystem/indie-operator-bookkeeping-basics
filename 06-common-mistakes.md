# Common mistakes

The bookkeeping errors that hurt solo operators most are predictable. Knowing them in advance is most of the fix.

## Mixing business and personal money

The biggest one. Shared accounts turn every statement into a puzzle and can undermine the legal protection of an entity. Separate accounts solve most other problems before they start.

## Not setting aside for taxes

Spending the full payment and facing a tax bill on money that is gone is the classic cash crisis. A holdback account that takes a percentage off every payment prevents it.

## Falling behind on records

Letting transactions pile up uncategorized turns a half-hour monthly task into a year-end reconstruction. The longer you wait, the less you remember and the more you guess. A monthly close keeps it small.

## Recording net instead of gross

Logging only the money that landed after fees hides both your real revenue and your deductible fees. Record the full amount earned and the fee as a separate expense.

## Throwing away the proof

Discarding receipts and records for expenses leaves you unable to back up a deduction if it is ever questioned. Keep digital copies, organized by month. Storage is cheap; reconstruction is not.

## Mixing accounting methods

Recording some things on a cash basis and others on accrual without a system produces books that do not reconcile. Pick one method and apply it the same way every time.

## Treating bookkeeping as a tax-season task

Books are a tool for running the business all year, not a chore for April. Operators who only look at the numbers at tax time miss the trends, the creeping costs, and the cash problems they could have seen coming.

## Waiting too long to ask a professional

General education like this gets you organized, but it does not replace advice for your specific situation. The cost of a question answered early is small next to the cost of a mistake compounding all year.
