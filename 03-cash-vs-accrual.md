# Cash vs accrual

Cash accounting records money when it actually moves. Accrual accounting records it when it is earned or owed, even if the cash has not changed hands yet. Most solo operators start with cash accounting because it is simpler and matches the bank account.

## Cash accounting

Under cash accounting, income is recorded when you receive the money and an expense is recorded when you pay it. Your books track the actual movement of cash, so they line up closely with your bank statement. For a one-person business with simple billing, this is usually the easiest method to keep and to understand.

## Accrual accounting

Under accrual accounting, income is recorded when you earn it, such as when you deliver the work or send the invoice, and expenses are recorded when you incur them, even if you pay later. Accrual gives a truer picture of performance over a period because it matches income to the work that produced it, but it is more complex to maintain and your books no longer mirror your cash balance directly.

## Which to use

Many solo operators use cash accounting because of its simplicity, and in some places it is allowed below certain size thresholds. As a business grows, carries inventory, or bills on terms, accrual can become more accurate or even required. Which method you may or must use can depend on your jurisdiction and situation, so this is a good question for a professional. The practical point is to pick one method, apply it consistently, and know which one you are using.

## Why consistency matters

Mixing methods, recording some things on cash and others on accrual without a system, produces books that do not reconcile and numbers you cannot trust. Whichever method you choose, apply it the same way every time. Consistency is what makes the records comparable across months and believable to anyone who reviews them.
