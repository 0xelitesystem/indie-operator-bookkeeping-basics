# Separate business and personal

Open a dedicated business bank account and run every business dollar through it. This single separation is the foundation of clean bookkeeping for a solo operator, and skipping it is the root of most of the mess people pay accountants to untangle later.

## Why it matters so much

When business and personal money share one account, every statement is a puzzle: which charge was a business expense, which was groceries, which transfer was income and which was you moving your own money. Reconstructing that at tax time is slow, error-prone, and stressful. With a separate business account, the statement is the record. Income comes in, expenses go out, and the account balance is your business cash.

## How to set it up

Open a business checking account, and a business card if you can. Route all business income into it and pay all business expenses from it. Pay yourself by transferring money from the business account to your personal account, and treat that transfer as a clearly labeled draw, not as an expense. The goal is that nothing personal ever touches the business account and nothing business ever touches the personal one.

## The liability angle

If you operate through a company such as an LLC, mixing personal and business funds can undermine the legal separation that protects your personal assets. Keeping the accounts distinct is part of maintaining that protection. This is a point worth confirming with a professional for your entity type, but the practice of not commingling funds is standard for a reason.

## Paying yourself

Decide how you take money out and keep it consistent. A regular transfer from business to personal, labeled as an owner draw, keeps the records clean. Avoid paying personal bills directly from the business account, even when it is convenient, because each one becomes a line you have to explain later. Move the money first, then spend it as personal money.

## Start now, clean up later is harder

The best time to separate accounts is before the next transaction. If your accounts are currently mixed, separating going forward still helps immediately, even if past records need work. Every day on a clean separate account is a day you will not have to reconstruct.
